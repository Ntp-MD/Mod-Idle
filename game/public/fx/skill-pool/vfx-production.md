## Spatial art direction — applies to every skill

The current art replacement pack is the live source shelf under `game/public/fx/skill-pool/`, with per-skill art, prompts, measured rectangles and QA stored beside it. Its twenty-one spatial replacements are active through additive runtime overrides; the original batch presets and PNGs remain available through `LEGACY_AUTHORED_VFX`. Combat behavior remains owned by canonical data and the combat implementation.

The standard ground footprint has **diameter 0.5m and radius 0.25m**. Its projected ellipse is the spatial ruler; actor silhouettes, heads, weapons and attack poses are independent art properties. Resolved footprint overrides must come from canonical data. World coordinates remain continuous meters.

Before generating or revising a skill, read its canonical delivery, origin, range, area radius and timing. Plan the assembled effect in meters: a radius of 3 meters gives a diameter of 6 meters, or 12 standard ground-footprint diameters. A radius is never a diameter. Target counts do not specify the number of visible particles or falling objects.

The assembled VFX must communicate the skill's full spatial action. Use the cast's frozen `SkillAreaSnapshot` and the arena ground projection for its center, orientation and extent. An area effect must visibly occupy its actual footprint, including empty ground between enemies. Distribution follows the area rather than the list of hit actors. A single enlarged stamp at the primary enemy does not establish area coverage. Resolve any mismatch between the actor ruler and the arena's meter projection explicitly before approving scale; increasing one sprite's size alone is insufficient. Line footprints map their source content start to the cast point and their source content width across the committed length, so a fissure reaches its full range without leaving the caster. Caster-origin areas sit on the ground layer at the caster's own feet, which the scene resolves from the drawn body (`bodyLift`) and hands down as `sourceGround`; a fixed pixel offset is only a preview fallback, never the arena anchor. Cone rows (`attack.venom_spray`, `attack.cleave`) are ground-layer sectors clipped to their committed angle. A row's `origin` decides which body its art hangs from: a melee line such as `attack.impale` starts at the caster and turns along the committed direction, while a projectile keeps `origin: 'target'` because that field owns where its impact lands, not where it launches. Rows whose atlas is frontal art still get their committed line or cone drawn on the ground layer from the frozen snapshot, so `attack.impale` and `attack.piercing_shot` show their full reach; a ground-plane strip would additionally let the spatial matrix own their length. `game/tests/ui/skill-vfx-spec.test.ts` pins coverage, movement, direction, anchor side and repeat count to the canonical rows for all 92 live skills.

- **Meteor:** a rain of multiple meteors descending from above with staggered arrivals and distinct impact points spread throughout the committed area. Show the area being bombarded, including its outer portions. Each meteor is one component of the whole shower; one comet centered on one enemy does not satisfy this direction.
- **Blizzard:** a sustained snow-and-ice storm with multiple falling ice elements, wind/snow motion and distributed ground impacts across the committed area. Preserve visible space between particles so actors and the storm's extent remain readable. One diagonal ice projectile or one burst on an enemy does not satisfy this direction.
- **Other skills:** derive the composition from their action. Rain/storm skills distribute moving elements throughout an area; ground bursts and novas expand across their footprint; sweeps and cones reveal their direction and spread; projectiles show their path and impact; self effects and auras follow their caster and intended ground plane. Preserve each skill's distinct identity.

Generation briefs must include the current chibi player and mob style references, the 0.5-meter ground-footprint ruler, the skill's meter dimensions, the projected footprint and the planned motion/composition. Generate separable fall, flight, impact or field components when the composition needs them; the atlas is source art for the assembled effect. Match the actors' crisp silhouettes and pixel clusters while keeping luminous cores readable.

Acceptance requires inspecting the complete animation with actual player/mob sprites in the integrated arena at the viewport specified by `AGENTS.md`. Check the actor-to-area size ratio, perspective, center-to-edge coverage, arrival timing, overlap and readability. A contact sheet or isolated effect preview alone cannot approve spatial scale. Cosmetic particles and staggered impacts consume the existing action's timing and add no gameplay hits. Reduced motion must retain a visible indication of the same area.

Each asset is first-party, generated separately with the built-in image generator. Store prompt, raw, QA and the explicit source rectangles beside the runtime art under `game/public/fx/skill-pool/<skill-slug>/v1/`. Runtime activation is controlled by `arena-authored-vfx.ts`.

Meteor selects the `components-v2` recipe through the same registry and scene, reusing the unchanged v1 rock, trail and impact PNGs. `game/src/ui/combat/fx-manifests/components-v2-manifest.json` owns source/version details, measured frame registration and the explicit diagonal screen-space motion. Each landing site shares the actors' ground-depth ordering; the quiet footprint remains on the ground layer. The spatial-v2 whole-field atlas remains the fallback. `python tools/check-authored-vfx.py --selected` checks selected source geometry and coverage; it does not certify motion or whole-roster arena acceptance.

Check real alpha, readable distinct silhouettes, consistent anchors and no visible content in cell gutters. The renderer explicitly discards alpha <=8/255 to remove generation noise; retain the original PNG and report raw alpha separately. `python tools/check-authored-vfx.py --write` finds empty separator scanlines above that threshold, rejects visible art at the atlas perimeter, and adds 64 pixels of virtual padding around source cells. Each source rectangle comes from actual image dimensions, never an assumed 256-pixel grid. Inspect motion and the integrated arena before handoff.

Aura atlases use all 16 frames as a continuous loop while enabled, independent of cast duration. `ArenaGroundAura.svelte` combines their texture with circular geometry using the presentation-only `groundAuraFootprint` in both the arena and preview. Its center follows the sampled actor's feet; its level ellipse matches the painted camera independently of facing and gameplay area geometry. Reduced motion holds a visible frame. Authored replacements suppress legacy supplemental skill art and actor marks; mob skills without authored replacements keep their existing effects.
